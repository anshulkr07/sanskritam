# Learn: where additional visuals would teach more

Reviewed 20 October 2026. This is a source and renderer audit, with inspection of existing phone screenshots. It is a recommendation, not a learner study or a new device-validation run. No artwork or app behavior was changed for this analysis.

## Finding

Yes: the next useful investment is explanatory visuals beside the teaching. The story already has broad illustration coverage; a scene that introduces a lesson does not necessarily demonstrate its grammar.

Across the five acts' unit content:
- All 139 story cards have an image.
- Only 3 of 188 core teaching cards have an image, all in Act 1.
- Another 91 optional depth teaching cards have no image.
- There are 158 table cards and 15 reading cards; 5 reading cards have images.
- There are 171 exploration cards. 123 are word-marking exercises; the remainder include mouth, letter, role, time, word-building, sandhi, compound, rhythm and derivation tools.

These card counts exclude the prologue and recovered tale panels. They therefore differ from the earlier complete-course count of 172 lesson-card paintings. Counts describe explicit image attachments, not whether a card already contains useful live visual structure.

## Recommended placements

32 opportunities below are grouped by teaching concept, not by required image file. One shared illustration can support several cards. P1 marks a recommended first batch of 12 concepts: 7 candidates for new explanatory illustration sets, and 5 for diagrams, reuse or enhancement of existing visuals. P2 means useful follow-up.

## Placement and presentation

- Put the explanatory visual on the relevant teaching or reading card. Existing CardBase/image support and the generic card renderer already permit an image on non-story cards; ordinary image placement does not need a new schema.
- Use one contrast per small phone panel. Keep the same cast, object and viewpoint when comparing quantity, case or voice. Change only the teaching variable.
- Use generated illustration for people, actions and meaning. Use live app text and code/SVG for letter assembly, arrows, brackets, grids, selection ranges and exact counting.
- Text in Learn images is permitted. Keep it brief, exact and proofread in the optimized phone file; repeat essential Sanskrit and explanations as real app content.
- Some existing four-column tables are 520 logical pixels wide because cells are 130px. On narrow phones they scroll horizontally. A second raster table would add duplication and shrink the writing; improve focus and navigation instead.
- Reuse suitable scenes in the four currently imageless optional reading episodes: who.reading, num.reading, past.reading and kss.reading. This is a reuse opportunity, not four required new paintings.
- Do not add images solely because a revision/check lesson currently has zero. Preserve text-based assessment and the policy against picture-choice exercises. Reusable mini references can help revision without repeating full scenes.

## Suggested execution order and verification

1. First batch: A1-01/02/03, A2-01/02/04/05/06, A3-01/02/03, A4-05. These are 12 teaching concepts, not a promised count of 12 generated files.
2. Next: compound/sentence structure and worked reading in Act 4, then the Act 5 selection/marker diagrams.
3. Add remaining semantic illustrations only after checking that each makes a particular distinction easier to see.

For each implemented visual, verify its exact source examples, participant roles and counts, Sanskrit glyphs, and any arrows or structural grouping. Check 320–430px phones, maximum text size, light/dark themes, tablet contain fitting, and failure/offline behavior. Confirm no answer is exposed in an assessment. Publish any new bitmap through the existing optimized phone/tablet WebP and immutable CDN workflow; discard temporary original PNGs only after delivery verification. Follow specs/artwork/ARTWORK.md.
