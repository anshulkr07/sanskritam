UNIT panini-gateway — Pāṇini's grammar

pg.shiva [scene] Fourteen lines of sound
s1 story [IMAGE pg-shiva-s1] The last alcove
t1 teach Fourteen lines | The Śiva sūtras list every sound of Sanskrit in fourteen short lines; tradition ascribes them to Śiva. Each line ends with a marker, a letter that is not itself a sound: ण् क् ङ् च् ट् ण् म् ञ् ष् श् व् य् र् ल्. They are Pāṇini's alphabet, arranged so that groups of sounds stand side by side.
tb1 table The fourteen lines | Each line, and the sounds before its marker | 14 rows x 3 cols
t2 teach Two-letter names | Pāṇini needs short names for groups of sounds. Take a letter and a later marker: the name stands for that letter and every sound between them, markers left out. So {{अच्}}, from अ to च्, is all the vowels; {{हल्}}, from ह to ल्, is all the consonants.

t1 teach Markers | A marker ({{इत्}}) is a sound that tells a rule what to do and then goes. 1.3.2: a nasalised vowel in the original teaching is one. 1.3.3: so is a last consonant. 1.3.9 drops it ({{लोपः}}). So {{तिप्}} becomes {{ति}}, and {{लँट्}} becomes {{ल्}}.

pg.prakriya [scene] Building a word
s1 story [IMAGE pg-prakriya-s1] The third alcove
t1 teach A word is built | To make {{भवति}}, Pāṇini starts from the root {{भू}} and adds what each rule asks, one at a time: the tense, the ending for “he”, the joining {{अ}}. The markers drop away. Then two sound changes. Each step has its sūtra number, and the steps run in order.

pn teach Pāṇini's order | Sūtra 8.2.1, {{पूर्वत्रासिद्धम्}}, opens the last three quarters of the Aṣṭādhyāyī. From there, each rule counts as not yet applied when an earlier rule looks at the word, and when a rule before it in that part does. So the sound changes there wait until the end, and the order of the rules decides the word.
