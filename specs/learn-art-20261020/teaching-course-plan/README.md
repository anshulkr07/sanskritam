# A teaching-first visual plan for the Sanskrit course

20 October 2026. Expanded after the owner's direction to prioritize Sanskrit teaching, allow ample visual material and multiple images on a card, and remove quantity as a limiting factor.

The previous 32-item audit was a shortlist of grouped opportunities. It was too narrow for a complete course redesign. This plan replaces that shortlist as the recommended scope.

## Recommendation

Build a visual explanation for every concept that benefits from one, including its meaning, form, conditions, contrasts and use in a complete sentence. Give difficult concepts several complementary representations. Keep the story as context and motivation, with instructional attention centered on Sanskrit.

The expanded main-lesson plan contains **574 named teaching modules across all 138 main lessons and all 44 units**. These are concrete proposed teaching jobs, such as a three-person comparison, a vowel-sign assembly, or a worked compound analysis. They are **not 574 missing images, 574 generated files, or a requirement to add 574 cards**. An illustration module can contain several pictures; a diagram can have several states; one reusable asset can serve several modules. Final image counts belong to the storyboards.

There is no 32-image ceiling or 12-concept stopping point. The goal is complete explanatory coverage. Pilot lessons should improve the reusable teaching formats before those formats are applied throughout the course.

## What the current content shows

The source audit covers the current 230 unit lessons, comprising 138 main lessons, 44 revisions, 44 checks and four optional reading episodes. The prologue and seven recovered tales bring the course total to 238 lessons and are tracked separately. The unit content has 2,098 cards and 137 declared skills.

The course already contains substantial teaching and practice. The imbalance is specifically in its imagery:

- All 139 unit story cards have image attachments; only 3 of 188 core teaching cards do.
- There are another 91 optional Pāṇini teaching cards.
- There are 158 live table cards. Many would benefit from illustrated headers, meaningful examples, pattern highlighting or focused comparisons.
- The 15 reading cards include only four designated optional reading episodes. Other reading cards are embedded in ordinary lessons.
- Of 171 exploration cards, 123 are word-marking activities. The remaining tools are useful but do not cover every explanatory need.
- All 932 practice cards already have an explanation field. The proposal is to improve explanations and transfer, not to imply that feedback is absent.
- Across 675 revision rows, 588 link back to a teaching card and **87 explicitly introduce something new**. New material needs a real explanation, example and contrast even when it appears inside revision.

Counts alone cannot prove teaching quality. A plain-text explanation can be excellent, and a fully illustrated card can still leave its concept unclear. The audit therefore maps actual learning operations, not just empty image fields.

## What each act needs

**Act 1: make the writing and sound systems visible.** Build a proper articulation atlas, aspiration/voicing comparisons, vowel-duration contrasts, dependent-sign assembly, virāma removal, conjunct families, repha versus rakāra, nasal and visarga contrasts, and a decoding procedure. Use exact font shaping and accurate anatomy. A learner should be able to explain how a printed word is read, then read a new one with the cues removed.

**Act 2: connect forms to meaning repeatedly.** This needs the most concrete illustration: action vocabulary, one/two/many, speaker/listener/referent, near/far, question types, negation, respectful address, ownership, kinship, location, source, destination, means, recipient and purpose. Give paradigms pictured context and complete sentences. Use the same scene to compare case roles, and different scenes to test whether the learner can transfer the rule.

**Act 3: distinguish what happened, who participates, and how the word is formed.** Provide contextual scenes for commands, wishes, possibilities, future plans, desire, obligation, causation and participial constructions. Use exact transformation diagrams for tense, class, root and ending changes. Explain lexical exceptions and the limits of simplified tense or voice descriptions. The active/passive distinction needs a stable event with changing grammatical presentation.

**Act 4: show the structure inside the text.** Every sandhi family needs boundary-focused examples and its conditions. Splitting needs candidate analysis, not a guaranteed one-step inverse. Compounds need head/referent links and nested grouping. Syntax needs clause diagrams. Metre needs aligned syllable and mātrā counts. Reading needs word correspondence across the source, padaccheda, anvaya and interpretation. These visuals should be as carefully designed as the paintings.

**Act 5: make technical grammar inspectable.** Highlight sound ranges and excluded markers; show why an it marker is identified and removed; explain each derivation stage and reconnect the resulting word to ordinary use. The Kathāsaritsāgara material needs a thorough guided reading with gradually reduced assistance. This is a gateway to Pāṇinian grammar, not complete mastery of the Aṣṭādhyāyī.

## Design lessons around explanation

A useful sequence is:

1. Establish the meaning with a clear scene or example.
2. Direct attention to the specific feature being taught.
3. Show how the Sanskrit expresses it.
4. Contrast it with the nearest confusing alternative.
5. Work through a complete example.
6. Let the learner complete part of another example.
7. Ask for independent interpretation or production.
8. Explain a mistake using the relevant visual.
9. Revisit the concept later with different vocabulary and reduced support.

This sequence is a design guide, not a compulsory nine-card template. Several steps may fit on one card; a difficult step may deserve several cards. Each new panel must answer a distinct learner question.

For the visual explanations, use adjacent live labels, small coherent steps and cues that direct attention to the relevant parts. These recommendations are consistent with Mayer and Moreno's research on multimedia explanations; that work does not establish a Sanskrit-specific optimum image count. [Research paper](https://carpentries.github.io/instructor-training/files/papers/mayer-reduce-cognitive-load-2003.pdf).

Gradually removing worked support is supported by research on transitions from examples to independent problem solving. Applying that approach to this course is a design proposal to test with learners. [Renkl, Atkinson and Maier](https://escholarship.org/content/qt81b9j9hs/qt81b9j9hs_noSplash_ef3e30a960524adc5157ece2d6fa3130.pdf).

Keep retrieval and later reassessment alongside the visuals. Roediger and Karpicke found delayed-retention benefits from retrieval practice with prose materials; that is a reason to measure retention here, not a guarantee of the same effect size in Sanskrit. [Original study abstract](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x/).

## Examples of the richer treatment

### One, two, many and agreement

A short explanation plus one story picture leaves several separate jobs to the learner. Give this topic:

- Three controlled illustrations: one reader, two readers, several readers.
- Live captions: बालकः पठति, बालकौ पठतः, बालकाः पठन्ति.
- A comparison table in which each pictured group aligns with its noun and verb.
- A diagram linking the two number-bearing forms.
- Another illustrated series with boats and अस्ति, स्तः, सन्ति.
- A worked correction in which only the mismatched form changes.
- The revision's two named subjects joined with च, showing how their total affects agreement.
- An independent Sanskrit response from a fresh context, followed by feedback that reveals the number relationship.

This can require multiple images on a comparison card and several cards within the topic. Reuse the images in revision, but use fresh contexts for transfer.

### Vowel signs and conjuncts

Give the learner large live components, the complete resulting glyph, an articulation/audio cue and a pictured example word. For कि, explicitly separate the sign's written position from the spoken sequence. For र्म versus प्र, show the two consonants in sound order and then their actual written shape. For stacked and ligature forms, use the current font's real shaping.

The pictures anchor meaning; the live glyph diagram explains spelling. A decorative board containing a whole alphabet cannot do all these jobs at phone size.

### Active, passive and causative

Keep one reading event visible for रामः पुस्तकं पठति and रामेण पुस्तकं पठ्यते. Link each word to the same participant or object, then change grammatical focus, case and verb highlighting. Explain the unchanged visible neuter form rather than suggesting that every role change produces a visibly different noun.

Introduce a separate teaching event for गुरुः बालकं पाठम् पाठयति. Show the additional causal participant and distinguish the learner's reading from the teacher's causing/teaching. Then work through the actual form changes. Additional verb types require separately checked role examples.

### Sandhi and reading

For a sandhi example, show the two complete words, enlarge only the joining boundary, name the condition, reveal the result and read it in a sentence. For splitting, present alternatives and use grammar and meaning to reject unsuitable ones.

For a verse, keep correspondence between original text, split words, prose order, grammatical relations and meaning. Fully explain one line, ask the learner to complete parts of the next, then provide an independent line. This needs several linked visual states, even if it requires no new narrative painting.

## Tables, graphs and multiple images

Support several complementary presentation patterns:

- **Image-and-word tables:** a picture in a header or row, large live Sanskrit, meaning, relevant features and a sentence. For example, one/two/many, action vocabulary, person/number and counted objects.
- **Controlled comparisons:** two or three panels with the same viewpoint and just the teaching variable changed.
- **Relationship graphs:** case roles, possession, agreement, antecedents and clause relationships.
- **Transformation tracks:** original form, relevant condition, intermediate stage and final form.
- **Decision diagrams:** sandhi conditions, blocked rules and candidate word splits.
- **Trees:** kinship, compound constituency and verified derivation dependencies.
- **Timing and metrical grids:** vowel duration, syllable weight, pādas and mātrā totals.
- **Aligned readings:** persistent word links across several analyses of the same source.
- **Post-answer explanations:** highlight the feature that caused an error, then show a parallel correct example.

Several images can share one card when they form one readable comparison. On a narrow phone, use a vertical sequence or a focused panel with a persistent caption when a grid would shrink the content. The course can have abundant visuals overall while each moment remains legible.

Do not fabricate measurement-looking sound graphs. Schematic duration bars should be labelled as schematic; waveforms or timing measurements must come from actual recorded audio.

## Revision, optional depth and assessment

**All 675 revision rows are in the review ledger.** The 588 linked rows can reuse teaching visuals and add recall prompts. The 87 `new_here` rows need full micro-explanations, not a formula attached to a decorative picture. Every new rule should have an example, its applicability and a contrast or exception where needed.

**All 91 optional depth teaching cards are separately inventoried.** Depending on the content, add a term map, condition diagram, source-linked example or exact derivation. Keep definitions and usage explanations distinct from derivations; the engine's output alone does not explain why a rule applies.

**All 44 checks need a feedback pass.** The learner's response can be a Sanskrit word, sentence, analysis, ordering or explanation. A pictured scene can establish context, with assistance revealed after the response. Preserve the existing rule against picture-choice exercises.

The 932 existing practice cards include 482 choice, 195 matching, 130 ordering, 68 typing and 57 engine-mode exercises. These modes do not by themselves prove the quality of the practice. Audit whether each skill is tested through meaning, form, new context and independent production. Add new tasks where a skill currently has only supported recognition.

Existing explanation text should remain available. A worked explanation is normal teaching content; the new visual teaching sequence should not depend on spending the separate daily answer-reveal hint.

## Additional needs for the larger ambition

More explanatory visuals can improve the course's design, but the broader ambition also needs:

- **A reading progression across the units.** Provide a short transfer reading or, in early script units, a decoding sequence after each unit. Four designated reading episodes already exist; audit how the other 40 unit checkpoints can use existing embedded readings or newly authored material.
- **Vocabulary progression.** Introduce meaningful illustrated words, reuse them across grammar contexts, and record what the learner is expected to know before a passage.
- **Production and explanation.** Ask learners to form sentences and explain contrasts, not only recognize a correct option. Pictures can be prompts while the answer stays in Sanskrit.
- **Pronunciation support.** Pair sound diagrams with checked audio and a way to compare the intended sound. Do not treat synthetic audio or a schematic mouth image as self-validating.
- **Longer independent reading.** Progress beyond isolated lines into passages with decreasing help and explicit source attribution.
- **Clearly stated scope.** The present Classical Sanskrit course and Pāṇini gateway should not be presented as exhaustive Vedic or advanced grammatical training. Those require separately designed, reviewed pathways.

## Check the explanation before drawing it

Some current beginner explanations are deliberately compressed. Before turning them into authoritative diagrams, check their scope and qualifications against the intended source and level. Examples needing particular attention are:

- Simple stem-plus-ending explanations versus complete derivations and indeclinables.
- Case labels versus semantic roles, including passive and noncanonical uses.
- Relative and correlative pronouns: shared reference and agreement must not be confused with mechanically identical case in both clauses.
- The contexts and historical/grammatical scope of tense descriptions.
- Lexical and contextual selection of the two verb-ending sets.
- Causative participant marking across different verb types.
- Sandhi conditions and exceptions, especially a schematic diagram that could otherwise imply a universal rule.
- Introductory śloka patterns versus permitted variants and actual scans.
- Articulation cues, which require phonetic accuracy rather than plausible-looking anatomy.

This is a targeted authoring review list, not a claim that the entire current course is unreviewed. Existing source approval does not automatically validate newly generated examples, new interpretations or every visual simplification.

## Product support required

The current schema gives each card one optional `image`. Table cells contain live Sanskrit or text, not image collections. The renderer shows the non-story card's image before its content. This supports an ordinary illustration attachment, but it does **not** yet provide arbitrary multi-image tables, figures interleaved with examples, or linked multi-panel explanations.

Add structured instructional blocks while preserving existing cards:

1. A figure with image/diagram, live caption, alt text, source and its teaching purpose.
2. A comparison group with several figures and responsive layout.
3. A semantic table whose cells can combine a figure with live text.
4. A step sequence with an explicit current stage and stable word/participant IDs.
5. A worked example with progressively hidden support.
6. Feedback blocks tied to the misconception or response, not just correctness.

Implement the data contract, validation, pack building, mobile types and renderer together. Keep existing image references compatible. New diagram data must be checked for valid word anchors and source forms. A generic image gallery alone would not supply the needed teaching behavior.

Use exact live Devanagari for critical labels and transformations, with optional IAST and accessible English/Hindi explanations. Text inside Learn artwork remains permitted and must be proofread. All essential Sanskrit, explanations and tables remain real app content too.

Load images as the learner reaches the relevant panels, reuse shared assets, and prefetch only a small upcoming set. Follow the existing optimized WebP/CDN policy. The reader's current On top / Own page / No image settings remain separate; the retired behind-text opacity layout is not part of this plan.

## Delivery and acceptance

Sequence the work by dependencies, with no arbitrary image cap:

1. Build the reusable multi-figure, comparison, diagram, table and worked-example support.
2. Implement representative lessons spanning script, case meaning, verb formation, sandhi and reading. Use them to settle legibility, teaching sequence and validation.
3. Apply the resulting formats throughout all 44 units and 138 main lessons.
4. Complete revision, optional depth, assessment feedback and unit reading transfer.
5. Test with learners who are new to Devanagari, readers of Devanagari who are new to Sanskrit, and returning Sanskrit learners.

A visual passes only if:

- Its depicted count, participants, action, direction, relationships and written forms match the intended teaching.
- The learner can identify what changes and why.
- A new example can be interpreted without the same image or labels.
- Conditions and exceptions remain visible at the appropriate stage.
- Phone text and figures remain readable at larger type sizes, with full image containment and accessible non-color cues.
- Source examples, grammar/engine checks where applicable, audio, glyphs and translations agree.
- Any new bitmap passes final phone/tablet delivery verification under the artwork policy.

Measure immediate understanding, independent use and later retention. Completion taps and image quantity are not sufficient evidence of mastery. This plan is a comprehensive authoring proposal; its teaching effectiveness still needs learner validation.
