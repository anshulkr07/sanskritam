# First 90 days of acquisition

## Monthly cash limits

Before revenue: ₹15,000/month: prizes/delivery ₹5,000; creators ₹4,000; paid acquisition ₹3,000; content production ₹1,000; reserve ₹2,000. These are caps, not required spending.

After revenue: ₹9,000 plus available cash contribution after store deductions, refunds, service costs, referral liabilities, fixed costs and credit-service reserves. Do not reinvest gross sales or spend the same prize/referral budget twice.

## Positioning and landing activity

Use two messages: “Understand the words you recite every day” and “Start Sanskrit with short, guided daily practice.” Every creative should demonstrate a real, available activity and point to that activity. Keep global availability, with paid experiments focused on one audience/country at a time.

Competition creatives must name the funded prize, dates, judging, adult eligibility, supported countries and free entry. Use AirPods only when the actual prize and delivery are secured. Membership or watching ads must never be presented as improving the chance to win.

## Two-week organic content calendar

| Day | Content | Invitation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Demonstrate one familiar shloka's word-by-word meaning | Try that verse's preview |
| 3 | Demonstrate one pronunciation distinction | Try the sample exercise |
| 5 | Show a beginner Sanskrit exchange | Complete the introductory activity |
| 8 | Explain a common misunderstanding with a cited text | Open the relevant reading preview |
| 10 | Show an actual reading/audio workflow | Explore a title, with clear unlock terms |
| 12 | Show a reviewed learner entry or teacher demonstration, with permission | Join a specific free-entry event |

Cross-post three useful short videos/week to Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts. Keep explanations clear in English for international audiences, with Hindi variants when useful. Do not imply guaranteed fluency, prizes or earnings.

## Creator pilot brief

Find one or two small creators whose audience actively asks Sanskrit or shloka questions. Ask for one accurate 30–60 second demonstration, transparent sponsorship disclosure, a paid-referral code and permission to reuse the video. Agree on price within the ₹4,000 total cap. Track creator cohorts independently; do not reward reviews or ratings.

No messages or payments to creators have been sent by this implementation. Publish campaigns and engage creators only through accounts and budgets you choose to use.

## Paid pilot and decisions

Use the strongest real demonstration on one channel, initially Instagram/Facebook. Cap the first test at ₹3,000. Track installations, completed first activity, day-7 return, purchases, renewals and refunds; compare prize entrants with learners acquired through teaching content.

Planning thresholds, not guarantees: 40% complete a first activity, 20% return on day 7, approximately 3% pay within 35 days, and acquisition cost recovered within three paid months. Small cohorts are noisy. Pause an experiment when it produces installs without meaningful activity or renewals.

Days 1–14: onboarding, staged payments and interviews with ten active users. Days 15–45: creator/paid/referral/event pilots after their operational release checks. Days 46–90: repeat channels that bring retained learners and customers. Do not expand countries or prize values simply because downloads increased.
