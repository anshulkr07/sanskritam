The experiment someone already ran for you

Vedapath ships 30+ sacred texts in 33 languages with word-by-word meanings, IAST, and AI chat — a broader feature set than most of its million-download rivals.

Comprehensive library 5K Vedapath downloads 30+ texts · 33 languages · word-by-word meanings · AI chat
Single beloved text 1M+ Bhagavad Gītā app downloads Several separate apps, each above a million, with one text

A 200× gap, with the better product on the losing side. This is not a marketing deficit that execution fixes — it is the market stating a preference plainly: people do not want a scripture library. They want the one text they already love, done well.

Do not build

A second app in the scripture-library category means competing against million-download incumbents on content volume — the single axis where a solo founder reliably loses — for a user who has already demonstrated they do not want breadth. The daily shloka inside the learning app captures the valuable part at no additional cost.

And the many-apps workaround is blocked

Shipping one app per text — a Shiva Purāṇa app, a Garuḍa Purāṇa app, built from a shared template — runs into a specific named rule on both stores.

Store policy
StoreRuleWhat it says
Apple Guideline 4.3 Names "multiple similar apps using a repackaged app template" as a spam factor. Directs developers to submit a single app with in-app purchases instead. Warns that spamming may lead to removal from the Developer Program.
Google Repetitive Content Prohibits "apps that merely provide the same experience as other apps already on Google Play" and "multiple apps with highly similar functionality, content, and user experience" under one account.
The asymmetric risk

Publishing the learning app and a fleet of scripture apps from the same developer account means a spam action against the fleet takes the learning app down with it. The side bet would be staking the validated business on the unvalidated one.

Demand is real. Payment is rare.

Every signal in this market splits the same way: attention is abundant and cheap, money is scarce and concentrated.

Sri Mandir raised over $50M, including a ₹175 Cr Series C led by Susquehanna with Peak XV and Elevation participating — and turned 40 million downloads into ₹18.5 crore. That is the ceiling-setter for the category, not a cautionary outlier.

Any plan that assumes downloads convert at Western SaaS rates fails here. At ₹46 of lifetime revenue per download, paid acquisition is arithmetically fatal.

The 10× that decides the market

ARPU — India ₹600–800 ≈ $7–9 Your audience
ARPU — abroad ₹7,000 ≈ $81 Your business

Published across the Indian devotional-tech sector. Sri Mandir draws roughly 20% of usage from diaspora in the US, UK, UAE, Canada, Australia and New Zealand.

One caveat, stated precisely: that ₹7,000 is driven largely by transactions — e-puja bookings and chadhava offerings — not content subscriptions. Devotional users pay for ritual acts performed on their behalf, not for reading. The figure does not transfer directly to a language app. What it does prove is that diaspora households spend real money on cultural connection, which is the same emotional purchase a heritage-language product sells.

Where the users actually are

The audience demonstrably consumes Sanskrit content on video, for free, at scale. That is the channel — not advertising, which the ₹46-per-download economics rule out entirely.

  1. Sponsor existing Sanskrit YouTube channels 1.3M subscribers already assembled on the largest channel alone, pre-qualified and demonstrably engaged. Cost per acquired user will be a fraction of paid social.
  2. Publish etymology content yourself "You already know 300 Sanskrit words" is true, shareable, and doubles as the app's onboarding. Costs time rather than money.
  3. Make Duolingo's refusal the headline They have publicly declined to build Sanskrit, citing low demand, while a petition runs against them. A clear antagonist, a demand proof point, and free press in one line.
  4. Temple weekend schools and cultural associations abroad Highest-intent, lowest-cost channel available, and the route to institutional licensing — the only path to the upside band.
  5. Partner with Samskrita Bharati rather than compete 10M+ people already trained and no strong digital product. A distribution channel, not a rival.

Verdict

Build — Sanskrit learning app

Demand is proven at 1.3M engaged subscribers. The lane is open — nobody teaches Sanskrit production, only reading. The tooling arrived this year. Diaspora ARPU makes it payable. Base case is a ₹1–3 Cr business owned outright.

Do not build — scripture library

Vedapath ran the experiment and returned 5,000 downloads against 1M+ for single-text apps, with a better feature set. Competition there is high, not low. The category monetises through ritual transactions you are not positioned to offer.
