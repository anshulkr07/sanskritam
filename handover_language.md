## 2. Why the design is what it is (measured 2026-10-02, run)

Four models were asked about 145 words whose origin had been read in a dictionary: 62 borrowed from
Sanskrit, 46 only related, 20 not from Sanskrit, 17 where dictionaries disagree. Each was also asked to
propose 20 new English words.

| | Gemini 3.8 Flash | Gemini 3.1 Pro | Sarvam conversations | Sarvam 105b |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Borrowed words recognised, with the right source (of 62) | 61 | 61 | 29 | 37 |
| Related words called related (of 46) | 46 | 46 | 10 | 41 |
| Non-Sanskrit words called borrowed (of 20) | 0 | 0 | 12 | 0 |
| Disputed words called borrowed and "certain" (of 17) | 9 | 7 | 9 | 3 |
| New proposals the dictionary confirms | 12 of 19 | 13 of 20 | 1 of 15 | no answer |
| Route names every stop the dictionary gives (of 36) | 26 | 28 | 6 | 8 |

What follows from it:

- Flash equals Pro here at about one-eighth of the cost.
- **About a third of what Flash proposes is wrong** by the dictionary (ginger, shampoo, chintz,
  camphor, beryl), and it marks most of those "certain". So nothing it proposes is shown unchecked.
- **Its routes differ from the dictionary's a quarter of the time.** So the route comes from the
  dictionary entry, not from the model.
- **A plain rule on the dictionary entry is enough to filter.** On the 90 English words of the gold
  list it passed 30 of 37 real loans, held 4 for a look, and passed none of the 53 others. On the 60
  words the models proposed it passed 25, and all 25 were right when read by hand.

Results: `backend/results/word_origins_bench/merged.json` (ignored by git).

## 3. What the learner sees

**Home.** One card in the Daily section, between the tithi card and the Śloka and Daily word pair.
It shows today's word in its own language, large, with the language named, its meaning, and one line
that says how it connects to Sanskrit, ending in the Sanskrit word in Devanagari. A faint first
letter of the Sanskrit word sits behind, the way the tithi card carries its numeral.

**The screen behind it**, top to bottom:

1. A deep panel with the Sanskrit word in Devanagari, its roman form, its meaning, and "Found in"
   with the text it is cited from, when there is one.
2. "The journey": a ladder from the Sanskrit word down to today's word, one stop per row, each with
   its language, its form and, when given, its meaning. For a related word the first join is not an
   arrow: it says "Same family", then the ladder continues from the relative.
3. A plain line naming the label: "Borrowed from Sanskrit", "From Sanskrit, through 3 languages" or
   "Same family as Sanskrit". When the dictionary hedges: "Dictionaries say probably".
4. "Also in": the other languages this Sanskrit word reached, as rows. Tapping one redraws the ladder.
5. The note, when there is one.
6. "Sources": the dictionaries behind this card, each opening its site.
7. "More words": earlier words, newest first. Tapping one opens the same screen for it.

There is no mockup. The look follows the Kāla hub (`mobile/src/screens/Kala.tsx`) and its shared
pieces in `mobile/src/kala/ui.tsx`.

## 4. Content: the files and their rules

### 4.1 Where content lives

Content is data, in `backend/app/seeds/word_origins/`, because the Dockerfile copies only
`backend/app`, `backend/migrations`, `backend/alembic.ini` and `frontend` into the image (read).
At boot the backend loads every `batch-*.json` there into the database. The database is a copy of
the files, never the other way round.

- `batch-NNNN.json`: approved content only. Batch 0000 is the hand-checked one.
- `rejected.json`: words that must never ship.
- Candidates that are not yet approved live outside the image, in `backend/results/word_origins/`
  (ignored by git).

### 4.2 The batch file

Read `batch-0000.json` for the shape. One item is one Sanskrit word with its routes:

```json
{
  "id": "sharkara", "status": "approved", "mw_key": "SarkarA",
  "sanskrit": {"dev": "शर्करा", "iast": "śarkarā"},
  "gloss": "grit, gravel; later ground sugar",
  "attested_abbr": ["AV.", "Kāv.", "VarBṛS.", "Suśr."], "attested": "Atharva-veda, as gravel",
  "note": "It first meant grit or gravel, and later ground sugar. ...",
  "routes": [{
    "id": "sharkara.de.zucker", "status": "approved", "lang": "de", "lang_name": "German",
    "word": "Zucker", "script": "latin", "roman": "", "gloss": "sugar",
    "kind": "loan", "confidence": "certain",
    "hops": [{"lang": "Arabic", "form": "sukkar"}, {"lang": "Italian", "form": "zucchero"}],
    "sources": ["pfeifer"], "lookup": "Zucker", "checked_on": "2026-10-01", "checked_by": "hand"
  }]
}
```

Rules, each one a test (section 5.6):

- **`mw_key` is the truth for the Sanskrit.** It is the stem in SLP1. `sanskrit.dev` and
  `sanskrit.iast` in the file are what `vidyut.lipi` gave for it; the loader derives them again and a
  test asserts the file agrees. Nobody edits Devanagari by hand.
- **`display_key`** is optional: the first-case singular a learner knows (`mAtA` for `mAtf`). It is
  allowed only when the Vidyut kosha confirms it as a first-case singular noun form of that stem.
  Eight items in batch 0 carry one (run). When absent, the stem is shown.
- **`kind`** is `loan` or `related`. Nothing else.
  - `loan`: the dictionary derives the word from the Sanskrit one. `hops` are the stops between, from
    the Sanskrit side outward. They exclude the Sanskrit word and the word itself. No hops means
    borrowed directly.
  - `related`: relatives from one older language, not a borrowing. `hops` may be empty, or may start
    with the relative the word comes from (Latin *ignis* for "ignite") and continue to the word.
- **`confidence`** is `certain` or `probable`. `probable` when any dictionary read hedges the step
  into Sanskrit. A word on which dictionaries disagree is not shipped at all.
- **`sources`** are keys of the licence registry (4.4). At least one. Every key must resolve.
- **`script`** is `latin` or `cyrillic` for now. **`roman`** is empty for `latin` and derived for
  `cyrillic` by one fixed table (5.3), never typed.
- **`attested`** is reader-facing and may be empty. `attested_abbr` holds the Monier-Williams
  abbreviations it rests on. Expansions come only from this table (confirmed against a transcription
  of the dictionary's own list, read): RV. Ṛg-veda, AV. Atharva-veda, VS. Vājasaneyi-saṃhitā,
  TS. Taittirīya-saṃhitā, ŚBr. Śatapatha-brāhmaṇa, Up. Upaniṣad, Nir. Nirukta, Mn. Manu's law-book,
  Yājñ. Yājñavalkya, MBh. Mahā-bhārata, R. Rāmāyaṇa, Suśr. Suśruta, Kāv. kāvya literature,
  Var. Varāha-mihira, Kathās. Kathā-sarit-sāgara. `R.` is the Rāmāyaṇa, not the Ṛg-veda. `L.` means
  lexicons only and is never shown as a text. An abbreviation not in this table leaves the line empty.
- **Wording is ours.** `gloss`, `note` and the hop glosses are written in our own words. No sentence
  from a dictionary is copied into a file. `gloss` is at most 8 words, `note` at most 40.

### 4.3 The schedule

A batch has a `start_date`. Its items are shown on consecutive days in file order, starting that day.
No two approved items may share a date across batches (a test). Batch 0 starts on 2026-10-15; the
owner sets this to the release date before deploying.

### 4.4 The licence registry

Add six rows to `CONTENT_SOURCES` in `backend/app/seeds/licences.py`. Licence text for all six:
`cited, not copied`. Titles are as each site prints them (read 2026-10-02).

| key | name | url |
|---|---|---|
| `mw` | Monier-Williams Sanskrit-English Dictionary (1899), Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries | `https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/` |
| `ahd` | The American Heritage Dictionary of the English Language, Fifth Edition | `https://ahdictionary.com/` |
| `pfeifer` | Wolfgang Pfeifer et al., Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Deutschen (1993), in the Digitales Wörterbuch der deutschen Sprache | `https://www.dwds.de/` |
| `duden` | Duden online | `https://www.duden.de/` |
| `vasmer` | Этимологический словарь русского языка Макса Фасмера | `https://gufo.me/dict/vasmer` |
| `platts` | Platts, A dictionary of Urdu, classical Hindi, and English (1884), Digital Dictionaries of South Asia | `https://dsal.uchicago.edu/dictionaries/platts/` |

Credit line pattern: "Word histories checked against <name>. No dictionary text is reproduced."
`taken`: "Word journey: the route of each <language> word." For `mw`: "Word journey: the Sanskrit
word, its meaning and where it is cited."

### 5.2 Loader

`backend/app/seeds/word_origins.py`, function `seed_word_origins(db)`, called from `run_seed()` in

### 5.3 Roman letters for Russian

`backend/app/services/romanize.py`, `romanize_ru(text)`: lower-case, then а a, б b, в v, г g, д d,

### 5.4 Service

`backend/app/services/word_origins.py`.

- `INCLUDE_RELATED = True`. When the owner says relatives are out, this becomes `False`: related
  routes are dropped everywhere and an origin left with no route drops out.
- `pick(origins, routes_by_origin, day)` is a pure function, so tests can call it with made-up rows:
  1. If an origin's `scheduled_date` equals `day`, that origin; headline is its first route.
  2. Otherwise take the origins scheduled before `day`, oldest first. None: nothing today.
     `n = len(them)`, `offset = (day - first scheduled date).days`. The origin is `them[offset % n]`,
     and the headline is its route number `(offset // n) % len(routes)`. So when the list runs out it
     comes round again showing each word's next language.
- `today_for(db, learner)`: the day is `local_date(learner.timezone)` (`services/localtime.py`), the
  same call the daily word uses (`services/daily_word.py:268`). Never server UTC.
- At most 4 SQL statements after auth: origins, routes of the chosen origin, sources. No query per row.

### 5.5 API

`backend/app/routers/word_origins.py`, `APIRouter(prefix="/word-origins")`, registered in
`app/main.py` with the others, tag "Word origins". Every route uses `require_learner`. No quota. No
writes: reading gives no XP and touches no mastery, like the story deck (`routers/story.py`).

Declare `/today` before `/{origin_id}`, or FastAPI reads "today" as an id.

- `GET /word-origins/today` → `{date, available, origin, headline_route_id}`. With nothing to show:
  `available: false`, `origin: null`, `headline_route_id: ""`, status 200.
- `GET /word-origins` → `{origins: [summary]}` for origins scheduled on or before today, newest first.
  A summary is `{id, sanskrit: {dev, iast}, gloss, languages: [..], scheduled_date}`.
- `GET /word-origins/{origin_id}` → an origin, or 404 if unknown, withdrawn or scheduled after today.

An origin:

```json
{
  "id": "sharkara",
  "sanskrit": {"dev": "शर्करा", "iast": "śarkarā"},
  "stem": {"dev": "शर्करा", "iast": "śarkarā"},
  "gloss": "grit, gravel; later ground sugar", "attested": "Atharva-veda, as gravel", "note": "...",
  "routes": [{
    "id": "sharkara.de.zucker", "lang": "de", "lang_name": "German", "word": "Zucker",
    "script": "latin", "roman": "", "gloss": "sugar",
    "kind": "loan", "relation": "travelled", "confidence": "certain",
    "hops": [{"lang": "Arabic", "form": "sukkar", "gloss": ""}], "sources": ["pfeifer"]
  }],
  "sources": [{"key": "mw", "name": "...", "url": "..."}, {"key": "pfeifer", "name": "...", "url": "..."}]
}
```

- `sanskrit` is the citation form when there is one, else the stem. `stem` is always the stem.
- `relation` is worked out, not stored: `related` for a related route; `borrowed` for a loan with no
  hops; `travelled` for a loan with hops.
- `sources` on the origin is `mw` plus every key its routes use, resolved from `content_sources` in
  one query.
- Pydantic models go in `schemas.py` beside `StoryCardResponse`.

## 7. Backend, part two: the pipeline that finds new words

Offline, run by the owner. Nothing here is on a request path. Build it after sections 5 and 6 work.

Scope of this part: **English loans**. That is the one check proven so far (section 2). German,
Russian and relatives are section 8.

### 7.1 The flow

```
propose   one Vertex batch job: Flash lists candidate English words
check     for each: read its American Heritage entry, apply the rule, gate the Sanskrit stem
write     one Vertex batch job: Flash copies the route out of the entry text and words the card
verify    code confirms every copied form is in the entry; anything else goes to "needs a look"
review    a sheet the owner reads: each card as it will appear, beside what the dictionary says
approve   the owner's yes writes batch-NNNN.json; the next deploy shows it
```

One command, subcommands: `./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m scripts.word_origins <step>`, with
`propose`, `collect`, `check`, `write`, `verify`, `review`, `approve`, `status`. Every step that
spends money or writes outside `results/` needs `--live`, as `scripts/challenge_batch.py` does. Each
step reads and writes one working folder, `backend/results/word_origins/<run id>/`, so a run can be
picked up after a job that takes hours.

Logic lives in `backend/app/services/word_pipeline.py` (pure, tested), `vertex_batch.py` and
`word_check.py`; the script is thin.

### 7.2 Vertex batch (proven 2026-10-02, run)

I ran one real job with these exact calls: three requests, `gemini-3.8-flash`, finished in 309
seconds (pending, queued at 32 s, running at 97 s, succeeded), three of three answered.

- **Credentials.** `vertex_credentials.json` in the repo root (ignored by git). Service account
  `video-pipeline@videos-498005.iam.gserviceaccount.com`, project `videos-498005`. Token:
  `google.oauth2.service_account.Credentials.from_service_account_file(path, scopes=["https://www.googleapis.com/auth/cloud-platform"])`,
  then `refresh(google.auth.transport.requests.Request())`. `google-auth` is in the venv (run); it
  arrives with `google-cloud-storage`, already in `requirements.txt`. Import it inside the function.
- **Bucket.** `videos-498005-batch`. The account can create, read, list and delete objects there and
  cannot list buckets (run). Use the prefix `sanskritam-word-origins/<run id>/`.
- **Upload** the JSONL:
  `POST https://storage.googleapis.com/upload/storage/v1/b/videos-498005-batch/o?uploadType=media&name=<object>`,
  `Content-Type: application/jsonl`.
- **One line** of the input file:

```json
{"key": "<your id>", "request": {
  "systemInstruction": {"parts": [{"text": "..."}]},
  "contents": [{"role": "user", "parts": [{"text": "..."}]}],
  "generationConfig": {"temperature": 0, "maxOutputTokens": 8000, "responseMimeType": "application/json"}}}
```

- **Create** the job:
  `POST https://aiplatform.googleapis.com/v1/projects/videos-498005/locations/global/batchPredictionJobs`

```json
{"displayName": "sanskritam-word-origins-<run id>-<step>",
 "model": "publishers/google/models/gemini-3.8-flash",
 "inputConfig": {"instancesFormat": "jsonl", "gcsSource": {"uris": ["gs://videos-498005-batch/<prefix>/input.jsonl"]}},
 "outputConfig": {"predictionsFormat": "jsonl", "gcsDestination": {"outputUriPrefix": "gs://videos-498005-batch/<prefix>/output/"}}}
```

- **Poll** `GET https://aiplatform.googleapis.com/v1/<job name>` every 30 seconds. Ends on
  `JOB_STATE_SUCCEEDED`, `JOB_STATE_PARTIALLY_SUCCEEDED`, `JOB_STATE_FAILED`, `JOB_STATE_CANCELLED`,
  `JOB_STATE_EXPIRED`. Save the job name to the working folder the moment it is created, so `collect`
  can find it in a later session. Google's page says a job can queue for up to 72 hours and most
  finish within 24 once running (read).
- **Output.** `outputInfo.gcsOutputDirectory` plus `/predictions.jsonl`. Each line has `key`,
  `request`, `response`, `status`, `processed_time`. **Lines come back in a different order: match by
  `key`.** A failed line has its error in `status` and an empty `response`. The reply text is
  `response.candidates[0].content.parts[*].text`.
- **Tokens.** `response.usageMetadata` gives `promptTokenCount`, `candidatesTokenCount` and
  `thoughtsTokenCount` (about 150 per request in the probe). Add them up per job and print them.
- **Price.** Google's page says batch is half the real-time rate (read). I did not see an invoice.
- Leave the objects in the bucket. Deleting is the owner's call.

`backend/app/config.py` gains `vertex_credentials_path` (default `../vertex_credentials.json`),
`vertex_batch_bucket`, `vertex_batch_prefix`, `vertex_location` (`global`) and `word_origins_model`
(`gemini-3.8-flash`).

### 7.3 Propose

One request per language (English only for now). The prompt measured in section 2, kept word for
word so the numbers still apply; it is `B_SYSTEM` and `B_USER` in `scripts/bench_word_origins.py`.
Ask for 60. The "do not use" list is every word of that language already live plus every word in
`rejected.json`.

Expect about two in three to survive the check, so 60 proposals give roughly 40 cards.

### 7.4 Check

`backend/app/services/word_check.py`. Move the working code out of the bench script, and make the
bench import it from here so there is one copy: `ahd_etymology`, `classify_ahd`, `fold`,
`parse_list`.

**The dictionary.** `GET https://ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=<word>` with a browser
`User-Agent`. The word history is the text of each `<div class="etyseg">` (run). A word with several
senses has several; all are kept.

- One request a second at most. Save every fetched entry in the working folder and never fetch a
  word twice in a run.
- An entry that only points elsewhere ("see LAC", "SANDAL + WOOD") is followed once: fetch the
  entry it names and use that text. Without this, lacquer, sandalwood and crimson fail (run).

**The rule**, on each segment:

| Entry says | Label |
|---|---|
| "from Sanskrit" or opens with "Sanskrit", no hedge before it | `sanskrit` |
| the same, with "probably", "perhaps" or "possibly" in the 160 characters before "Sanskrit" | `sanskrit_hedged` |
| Sanskrit named only as "akin to" | `akin_only` |
| no Sanskrit | `not_sanskrit` |
| no entry, or no word history | `no_etymology` |

Only `sanskrit` goes on automatically. `sanskrit_hedged` goes to "needs a look". The other three are
rejected and appended to `rejected.json` with the reason.

**The Sanskrit gate.** Take the form the entry prints after "Sanskrit" (it is the first case:
*yogaḥ*, *jaṅgalam*, *śarkarā*), turn it to SLP1 with `vidyut.lipi`, drop a final `H` or `m`. That
stem must be in the Vidyut kosha's stem list. `Kosha(...).pratipadikas()` gives 205,233 stems in
about 7 seconds (run); read each stem's text out of the entry. On 36 forms the dictionary printed,
34 were found, and seven invented sources from the Sarvam run all failed (run). The kosha is not
complete (*rājā*, *sthaga*, *dvār* are missing), so a miss means "needs a look", not rejection. The
found stem is the item's `mw_key`.

**Acceptance for this step** (run it live once, it is free):
`python -m scripts.word_origins selfcheck --live` fetches the English words of
`tests/fixtures/word_origins/gold.json` and must show: none of the 24 related, 17 non-Sanskrit and
12 disputed words labelled `sanskrit`; at least 30 of the 37 borrowed ones labelled `sanskrit`.
Those are the figures I measured on 2026-10-02 before the cross-reference step existed (30 passed,
4 hedged, 3 lost to cross-references).

### 7.5 Write

A second batch job, one request per surviving word. The model is given the entry text and the stem,
and told to copy, not to recall:

- System: "You copy facts out of a dictionary entry. Use only the entry text you are given. Copy
  every word form exactly as printed. Reply with one JSON object and nothing else."
- User: the word, its language, the Sanskrit stem in roman letters, the entry text, and the keys
  wanted: `"sanskrit_form"` (as printed), `"hops"` (the stops between the Sanskrit word and this
  word, from the Sanskrit side outward, each `{"lang", "form"}` as printed), `"hedged"`,
  `"gloss"` (the meaning of the word today, at most 8 words, your own words),
  `"sanskrit_gloss"` (the meaning the entry gives for the Sanskrit word, at most 8 words),
  `"note"` (at most 40 words, your own words, only what the entry says; `""` if there is nothing
  worth saying).

This prompt is **not measured**. Measure it on the first run: `verify` prints how many replies pass.

### 7.6 Verify

Code, no model. A reply is accepted only if all of these hold; otherwise the word goes to "needs a
look" with the reason:

1. `sanskrit_form` and every hop `form` occur in the entry text (compare after Unicode NFC, case
   ignored).
2. Every hop `lang` occurs in the entry text.
3. In an American Heritage entry the forms run from the word back to the Sanskrit, so the hop forms
   must appear in the entry in the reverse of their order in `hops`.
4. `hedged` agrees with the rule in 7.4.
5. `gloss` and `note` hold no em dash, keep to their lengths, and share no run of 8 or more words
   with the entry text.
6. The word is not in `rejected.json` and not already live.

Then build the item: `mw_key` from 7.4, `kind: "loan"`, `confidence` from the label, `sources:
["ahd"]`, `lookup`, `checked_on` today, `checked_by: "rule"`, `attested` empty.

### 7.7 Review and approve

`review` writes `results/word_origins/<run id>/review.md`: for each candidate, the card as the
learner will see it (word, meaning, each stop, the label, the note), then the entry's own text and
its address, then why it is in "needs a look" if it is. Passed candidates first.

`approve --run <id> --except word1,word2 --start YYYY-MM-DD` writes the next `batch-NNNN.json` from
the passed candidates minus the exceptions, plus any "needs a look" words named with `--also`.
Without `--start` the batch begins the day after the last scheduled date. It refuses to write if a
date would collide or a test in 5.6 would fail on the new file. Words in `--except` go to
`rejected.json` only when the owner adds `--reject`; a word can be held back without being banned.

The owner then commits and deploys. Until then nothing changes for learners.

### 7.8 Tests: `backend/tests/test_word_pipeline.py`

No network. Feed the functions made-up text in the dictionary's shape; do not paste dictionary text
into the repo.

1. The rule: one made-up segment for each of the five labels, a hedge 150 characters before
   "Sanskrit", and a hedge after it (which does not count).
2. The cross-reference step is followed once and not twice.
3. The Sanskrit gate: a printed form ending in ḥ, one ending in m, one in ā; an invented form fails.
4. Verify: a form not in the entry fails; hops in the wrong order fail; a note that copies eight

5. Vertex client with a mocked transport: the upload, the job body, polling to each end state, and

6. `approve` on a made-up run writes a file that passes every test in 5.6, and refuses a date
